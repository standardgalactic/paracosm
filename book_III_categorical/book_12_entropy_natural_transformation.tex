\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}

Book 11's Chapter 5 previewed a natural transformation $\SEntropy: \PhiField \Rightarrow \vField$, establishing only enough of its structure to motivate the categorical vocabulary that book was introducing. The present book redeems that preview in full. Here, entropy is formalized as the natural transformation between the scalar potential functor and the vector flow functor within the category $\mathbf{RSVPField}$, and the second law of thermodynamics is shown to be nothing other than a naturality condition: entropy mediates every lawful relation between potential and movement, and every failure of that mediation to remain natural is, in the vocabulary this book develops, exactly what informational or ethical friction consists in. Entropy, by the end of this book, is established not as loss but as the structural continuity that allows systems to learn, evolve, and care.

\chapter*{Diagrammatic Overview}

The passage from Book 11 to the present volume is the passage from the bare category $\mathbf{RSVPField}$ to the specific natural transformation $\SEntropy: \PhiField \Rightarrow \vField$ that book only sketched. Every commutative diagram in this book has $\SEntropy$ at one of its arrows, and the reading throughout is consistent: where the diagram commutes, entropy has done its mediating work correctly, and where it does not, the discrepancy is heat.

\chapter*{Reading Note}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Reading Note}

Entropy is not a quantity in this book; it is a relation that remains natural under change. The reader accustomed to treating entropy as a number attached to a state should expect that habit to be actively unlearned across these twelve chapters, replaced by the categorical habit of asking, of any given entropy claim, which naturality square it corresponds to.

\part{Foundations of Functorial Thermodynamics}

\chapter{The Scalar and Vector Functors}

The scalar functor $\PhiField: \mathbf{RSVPField} \to \mathbf{SmoothMan}$ and the vector functor $\vField: \mathbf{RSVPField} \to \mathbf{VecBundle}$ map each object of the category Book 11 constructed to, respectively, the smooth manifold carrying its potential structure and the vector bundle carrying its flow structure. $\PhiField$ is interpreted here as capacity, what a system could realize, and $\vField$ as expression, what a system is currently realizing, and both functors are established to preserve the curvature-compatible morphisms of $\mathbf{RSVPField}$, carrying a curvature-preserving map on objects to a corresponding structure-preserving map on the respective target categories.

The philosophical reading offered at the close of this chapter treats potential and flow as two views of the same care: $\PhiField$ and $\vField$ are not independent functors describing unrelated aspects of an object but two functorial images of a single underlying object, related, as the rest of this book shows, by exactly the natural transformation that gives this book its title.
